# Avoid LLM Tells

These patterns make prose read as machine-generated. Remove them in a final
editing pass.

## Dashes first

- Do not use `—` (em-dash) or `–` (en-dash). The hard cap is zero, headings
  included.
- Replace with a comma, colon, parentheses, or two sentences.
- Do not use `--` as a substitute.

Rewrite a dashed clause as its own sentence or join it with a comma. "The portal
is fast, and it is themeable" reads better than the dashed version.

## Always-replace words

delve -> explore; leverage (verb) -> use; robust -> reliable; seamless ->
smooth; utilize -> use; landscape (metaphor) -> field. For tapestry, synergy,
game-changer, cutting-edge, and embrace (metaphor), say the concrete thing.

## Constructions to avoid

- "It's not just X, it's Y" and "not only X but Y." Rewrite as a direct
  statement, at most one per document.
- Hollow hedges and intensifiers: genuinely, truly, quite frankly, it's worth
  noting that, it's important to note, could potentially. Keep one hedge at most.
- Vague endorsements ("worth reading"), chatbot artifacts ("Great question!",
  "I hope this helps!"), and template openers ("In today's X," "When it comes
  to," "Whether you're X or Y").
- Cutoff disclaimers and unfilled placeholders left in the text.

## Flag-in-clusters words

Any one may be fine; if two or more cluster, rewrite the paragraph plainly:
harness, navigate, foster, elevate, unleash, streamline, empower, bolster,
resonate, revolutionize, facilitate, underpin, ecosystem, myriad, plethora.

## Rhythm

Prefer plain copulas ("is," "has") over "serves as," "boasts," "features." Do
not synonym-cycle; repeat the clearest term. Vary sentence length; machine prose
is metronomic.

## Pass order

1. Delete every em-dash and en-dash, rewriting the sentence around it.
2. Replace always-replace words.
3. Cut hedges, intensifiers, and template openers.
4. Break up any run of three same-length sentences.
